Help

How the {{ product }} Academy works

Everything runs in your browser — nothing to install. This guide covers how the catalog is organised, what you see inside a lab, and how to manage your sessions.

The big picture

Tracks, labs and trophies

{{ icon('layers')|safe }}

Tracks

A track is a themed learning path — a group of related labs in a sensible order (for example Foundations, Developer, Security). On the catalog each track is a collapsible section.

{{ icon('rocket')|safe }}

Labs

A lab (or workshop) is a single hands-on exercise with its own private environment. Each shows its difficulty and rough duration. Work through them in order — later labs build on earlier ones.

{{ icon('trophy')|safe }}

Trophies

Finish a lab and you earn its trophy. Finishing has to be recorded — see the note below for what does that. Your Trophies page shows what you've earned and what's left in each track.

{{ icon('info')|safe }}

A terminal lab is only marked complete when you submit its feedback form. Working through every page does not record it, and neither does closing the session window — the trophy appears when the form is submitted. The Feedback tab is always there in the lab window; it takes about fifteen seconds.

A console lab is the exception: pressing Finish in the guidance box records your completion, and the feedback form that follows is optional (but still welcome).

Your progress is tied to your login, so trophies and the “Continue where you left off” shortcut follow you across visits and devices.

Two kinds of lab

Terminal labs and console labs

Every lab teaches with one of two formats. The catalog tile tells you which before you start, and the two are meant to be used in that order: you learn a concept on the command line, then you apply it in the graphical console.

{{ icon('terminal')|safe }}

Terminal lab

The classic format, and where the teaching happens. Instructions on the left, a terminal, editor and console on the right. You read what a Deployment is and why it exists, then create one yourself with oc. Most labs are this kind.

{{ icon('monitor')|safe }}

Console lab

A guided tour of the real OpenShift web console. There are no instruction pages: the next step is highlighted directly on the page you are looking at, with a short explanation beside it. These assume you already met the concepts in a terminal lab — they show you where the same things live in the graphical interface.

A console lab opens the OpenShift console rather than the lab window, so it looks different from the screenshots further down this page. Your project and its resources are created for you before it starts.

Where your lab runs

Namespaces and virtual clusters

Whichever format a lab uses, it gets a private, throwaway environment on the shared cluster. Labs differ in how much of a cluster they need, which is shown as a badge on the lab's page.

{{ icon('box')|safe }}

Namespace

A dedicated project (Kubernetes calls it a namespace) on the shared cluster — the same unit of isolation you get on the real platform. You can create anything a normal user may create inside it, and nothing you do is visible to other learners. This mirrors how you work on DCS day to day.

{{ icon('layers')|safe }}

Virtual cluster

A whole Kubernetes control plane of your own, running inside the shared cluster. Labs use this when the exercise needs cluster-wide rights — installing an operator, editing cluster-scoped objects — that nobody can safely be given on a shared cluster. It takes a little longer to start.

Both are deleted when your session ends. Nothing you build in a lab survives, and nothing you break there can affect anyone else.

Getting started

From catalog to running lab

1
Pick a lab

Browse or search the Catalog. Use the difficulty filters, or open a track and pick a lab to see its full description.

2
Start session

Click Start session. It opens in a new browser tab so the catalog stays open behind it. A progress screen shows your private environment being provisioned (a workshop pod, and for some labs a virtual cluster).

3
You're dropped into the lab

When it's ready you're taken in automatically. If the progress bar seems stuck for a while, a page refresh resumes right where it left off.

Inside a lab

The session window

The screen is split in two: instructions on the left, your work area on the right. You read a step on the left and carry it out on the right — usually by clicking the coloured boxes in the instructions rather than typing (see quick actions).

Instructions toolbar (top-left)

  • {{ icon('circle-check')|safe }} Home — jump back to the lab's first page.
  • ← Back / → Forward — move one page at a time through the lab.
  • {{ icon('list-checks')|safe }} Contents — the page list; jump straight to any section.
  • Print — a printable view of the instructions.

Session controls (top-right)

  • {{ icon('clock')|safe }} Countdown — time left before the session expires. Sessions are generous but not forever; finish or restart before it runs out.
  • {{ icon('refresh')|safe }} Reload — refresh the workspace if a tab looks stuck.
  • {{ icon('grid')|safe }} Fullscreen — expand the work area to the whole window.
  • {{ icon('logout')|safe }} Leave — return to the portal. The session keeps running; reopen it any time from My sessions.
  • {{ icon('cog')|safe }} Menu — extra options for the session.
The work area

Terminal, Console and Editor

Switch tabs with the headers at the top of the right pane. Which tabs appear depends on the lab; most have all three.

{{ icon('terminal')|safe }} Terminal

A shell in your environment, starting in ~/exercises. Many labs use a split terminal — an upper and a lower one (numbered 1 and 2 in the screenshot above) — so you can, for example, watch something in one while you make a change in the other. Commands use the OpenShift CLI, oc.

{{ icon('monitor')|safe }} Console

A visual web console for your session's namespace — browse Deployments, Pods, Services, ConfigMaps and Secrets without the command line. Labs send you here to see what you just created.

{{ icon('edit')|safe }} Editor

A full VS Code editor open on ~/exercises for viewing and editing files. Use the Explorer on the left to open files; many clickable actions in the instructions open and edit files here for you.

Inside a console lab

Guided tours of the OpenShift console

A console lab has none of the tabs above. Instead the OpenShift web console opens with your project already selected, and two boxes appear on top of it.

  • {{ icon('circle-check')|safe }} The step box sits next to whatever you should look at, which is outlined on the page. It says what to do and why it matters.
  • {{ icon('arrow-right')|safe }} Continue moves to the next step. You do not need it when you perform the step yourself — the lab notices and moves on — but it is always there, so a step can never trap you.
  • {{ icon('chevron')|safe }} Back returns to the previous step if you want to read it again.
  • {{ icon('list-checks')|safe }} The progress box in the corner shows the step count and the state of your project. Stop ends the tour but leaves your environment running.

Finish the last step and press Finish: that records your completion and shuts the environment down. Everything you see is the real console acting on real objects, so anything you learn here transfers directly to your own projects.

You rarely type

Quick actions — the clickable boxes

Wherever the instructions show a coloured box, it is a button: click it instead of typing. This is how a lab is meant to be worked through — read the step, click the box.

{{ icon('terminal')|safe }}

Run a command

Click a command box and it types and runs in the terminal for you.

{{ icon('edit')|safe }}

Open or edit a file

Click to open a file in the editor and apply the change — you watch it happen, no hand-editing.

{{ icon('list-checks')|safe }}

Verify

A check box runs a test to confirm the step worked before you move on.

{{ icon('grid')|safe }}

Open a tab

Switches the right-hand side to the Terminal, Console, Editor or Slides. Only one tab shows at a time — click the Terminal tab header to come back.

If a box doesn't seem to do anything, make sure the right tab (usually Terminal) is visible, then click again.

Managing your work

The session manager

My sessions (top-right menu) lists every lab you currently have running.

{{ icon('rocket')|safe }} Open
Jump back into a running session — the same environment you left, with your work intact.
{{ icon('refresh')|safe }} Delete
End a session completely and free its resources. This removes the environment, so anything unsaved is lost — but it also lets you start that lab fresh next time instead of resuming a broken or half-finished one. You'll be asked to confirm.

You can run a limited number of sessions at once. If you hit the limit, the portal offers to free one so you can start another.

Tips

Getting the most out of a lab

  • Follow the order. Steps build on each other, and verify checks gate progress on purpose.
  • Watch the visible tab. Running a command switches you to the Terminal — switch to the Console when a step points there.
  • Take your time. Read the why, not just the what.
  • Stuck? Re-read the last expected output, confirm the right tab is visible, and re-run the action.
  • Tell us how it went. The feedback form at the end takes 15 seconds, is what earns your trophy on a terminal lab, and shapes the next labs.
